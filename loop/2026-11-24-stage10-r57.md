# The VM answers an operator of two builtin numbers directly

Toward the VM as the default execution (note 558's first item for this stream). After #184 and round 54, six of ruby's `benchmark/app_*` and `so_*` programs still took the VM more than 1.05 times the evaluator's instructions. The slowest was app_mandelbrot at 1.16 times.

**What the VM paid.** An operator the VM's own fast paths did not take went to `vm_call`'s last arm: a scope from the pool (`vm_scoped`), the two values bound in it as hidden locals, and the parser's `EBin` built over them and evaluated. The fast paths took two Integers, two Floats, and a Float with an Integer under `+ - *`. Everything else paid the scope:
- `z * z` of two Complexes;
- `z.abs > 2`, a Float against an Integer;
- `dx / 50.0`, an Integer divided by a Float;
- a Rational's and a Bignum's arithmetic.

Net of the loop around it, `i / 50.0` cost 10.5k instructions on the VM and 5.7k on the evaluator. `z * z` cost 21k against 15.5k.

**The change.**
- `vm_call` has an arm for `+ - * / < <= > >=` of two builtin numbers: an Integer, a Bignum, a Float, a Rational, or a Complex with no object in it. It calls `eval_bin_prim`, which is where `EBin`'s arm ends for such a pair. `vm_num_bin_ok` says when nothing comes before that end:
  - no TracePoint and no refinement in effect;
  - the operator is not undefined or aliased anywhere;
  - the receiver has no subclass tag;
  - the receiver's class does not define the operator.
- A Complex with an object in it, a program's own Numeric and an object that coerces are left to `EBin`, as before. The argument must be a builtin number, so no `coerce` is skipped.
- `flt_fast_op` takes an Integer divided by a Float. It was left out because `int_ops_plain` did not ask after `Integer#/`; round 54 made it ask.

## Three differences from ruby found by the corpus program

Corpus 813 runs every pairing of the five kinds of number under the eight operators. It found three differences, on both executions and on main alike.

**A Fixnum times a Complex.** `3 * Complex(1.5, -2)` was `(4.5-6.0i)`. Ruby's is `(4.5-6i)`: numeric.c's `fix_mul` hands the pair to `rb_complex_mul(y, x)`, which multiplies each part by the Integer, so an Integer part stays one. Here the Integer was made a Complex first, and the imaginary part became `3 * -2 + 0 * 1.5`, a Float. `cpx_arith_f` now multiplies the parts for a machine Integer on the left. A Bignum, a Float and a Rational on the left are coerced first in ruby too, and are unchanged.

**An Integer or a Float compared with a Complex.** `1 < Complex(1, 1)` was ArgumentError "comparison of Integer with Complex failed". Ruby's is NoMethodError "undefined method '<' for an instance of Complex": the pair is coerced to two Complexes, and Complex has no ordering. A Rational on the left is Comparable's failed comparison in ruby, and was already.

**The other side of a failed comparison.** `1 < nil` said "comparison of Integer with NilClass failed". Ruby's `rb_cmperr` writes a special constant as itself: "with nil", "with true", "with :s". `cmperr_cls` does so now, as `cmp_other_name` already did for sort, min and max. A Bignum, a Rational and a Complex on the other side are named by their class, as ruby names them. Before, `"a" < 2**70` wrote out the number.

**Left, as on main:**
- The NoMethodError of `1 < Complex(1, 1)` has the caller as its first backtrace frame; ruby's first frame is `Integer#<`.
- With `Rational#-` redefined, `1 - r` does not reach it. Ruby coerces the Integer and calls the program's method. The same holds for `1 - z` with `Complex#-` undefined, which ruby refuses.
- `[1, true].max` names its sides the other way round ("comparison of TrueClass with 1 failed").

## What is left of the six

app_mandelbrot is the one this round moves. The other five have no cost that is the VM's alone and large. Net of an empty loop, on both executions:
- a call of a method written in Ruby costs about 26k instructions;
- a call of a block costs about 7k;
- `a.at(0)`, a builtin, costs about 12k.

A sample of the builtin call has 47% of its time in string comparison and hashing: the dispatcher's chain of names, the arity memo's keys, and `vm_binop`'s list of twenty operators, which the VM asks for every call with one argument. That is the next round's.
