# Mere 9dec2ff6: a name tested against a literal no longer calls a function

Round 57 left five of ruby's benchmark programs where the VM takes more than 1.05 times the evaluator's instructions, with no large cost that is the VM's alone. This round measures what a call costs on both executions.

**What a builtin call spends.** Net of the loop around it, `a.at(0)` cost about 12k instructions on either execution. A sample of 3 million of them on the VM had 44% of its time in two functions of Mere's runtime:
- 26% in `__lang_str_eq`, the string equality, which the C compiler had left out of line;
- 18% in memcmp and the linker's stub for it, which `__lang_str_eq` calls whenever the two lengths agree.

That was about 400 calls of `__lang_str_eq` for one Ruby call, and about 75 of them reached memcmp. The dispatcher tests the method's name against a chain of literals; `"at"` has the length of `"=="`, `"<<"` and `">="`.

**The change in Mere** (merelang/mere #90, 9dec2ff6). `__lang_str_eq` is `always_inline` and compares the first byte before memcmp. A test that fails on the length is two loads and a compare in the caller. One that fails on the first byte never calls memcmp. An empty string is answered by its length before any byte is read.

`MERE_REF` moves from 0c083af0 to 9dec2ff6. Mere's own change between the two, besides this, is #89, which is RISC-V only.

**In the VM.** `vm_call` asked `vm_binop` of every call with one argument: is the name one of twenty-one operators? It asked `vm_unop` of every call with none. Both now ask the name's shape first (`vm_op_shaped`): one to three bytes, not starting with a letter.

The evaluator gains more than the VM where the program is operators. Its `EBin` arm and `eval_bin_prim` test the operator's name against their chains for every operation. The VM's fast paths answer most of them by the operator's bytes, with no name tested. So so_spectralnorm's ratio is now 1.13 times, though both executions are faster.

The binary grows from 14.18 MB to 14.74 MB: 3,448 tests of a string are inline now.

The builtin call is the one that was made of name tests. A Ruby method's call and a block's call are frames, and are as they were.

**Also measured: what entering a VM frame costs.** A method whose body is one constant (`def m(a, b); 1; end`) is answered without entering the VM, at 30.7k instructions a call with two arguments. A body that reads a parameter (`a`) costs 32.4k, and one that writes a local (`x = 5; 1`) 32.9k. The evaluator's are 31.8k and 31.9k. The 1.7k to 2.2k between them is the frame's entry: the pooled stack and registers, the handler that gives them back on a raise, the region, the value copied out. A sample shows no single part of it over 0.4k. It is recorded here and not changed.

A sample of the builtin call after the change has the string-keyed Map's lookup on top (15%, with its hash 5% and its memcmp 6%): the frames, and the memo tables asked by a class and a name. That is the frame rework's (note 552 §17), not a comparison to move.

## Recorded, from the reviews of #199 and #202

These are on main, on both executions, and are not this round's.

- **A method added to an ancestor comes before a builtin class's own.** After `module Comparable; def <(o) = :c; end`, `1 < 2`, `1.5 < 2` and a Bignum's `<` answer `:c`. In ruby only a Rational's does, as Integer and Float define `<` themselves. `class Numeric; def -(o)` likewise takes `5 - 2` and `Complex(1, 1) - 1`.
- **A program's `Rational#<=>` is not asked by Rational's `<` and `>`.** With `def <=>(o) = 1`, ruby's `Rational(1, 2) < 1` is false.
- **`2 * Rational(1, 2)` does not reach a redefined `Rational#*`.** Ruby's Integer#* coerces and calls it. Round 57 recorded the same for `-` and for an undefined `Complex#-`.
- **A class reopened through a constant that names it.** `N = RubyVM::AbstractSyntaxTree::Node; class N; def dup = :d; end` does not reopen Node, and neither does `B = A; class B`. #197 has the fix for classes and modules.
