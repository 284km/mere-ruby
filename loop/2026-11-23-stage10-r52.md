# Integer's >> << & | ^ % / of two Integers by the fast path, and a send of one on the VM answered without a scope

Toward the VM not being slower than the evaluator (stage 10's third condition). Of ruby's app_ and so_ benchmarks, six were slower on the VM than on the evaluator, so_nsieve_bits the most (1.29x the evaluator's instructions).

## What so_nsieve_bits measures

Its inner `p.step` never runs on ruby 4.0: `items[i][j] == 1` compares a String with 1. So it measures a block called 2.5M times with `p >> 3`, `p & 7`, two `String#[]` and an `==`. Small programs, in instructions retired (300,000 calls):

| | evaluator | VM |
|---|---|---|
| an empty block | 2.58 G | 2.70 G |
| `p >> 3; p & 7` | 5.94 G | 9.21 G |

- int_fast_op, the two-Integer path that both executions take, answered only `< > + - * <= >= == !=`. The other operators went the general way. On the evaluator that meant the operator chain of EBin's arm. On the VM, `>>` is a send, and vm_call_in made the parser's EBin of it in a scope of its own (vm_scoped).
- int_fast_op now answers these as well:
  - `& | ^`;
  - `>>`, arithmetic: floor division by 2^n, and the sign for a shift past the width;
  - `<<`, while the answer fits;
  - `%` and `/` of a non-negative by a positive, where floor and truncation agree.
- Everything else goes the general way as before: a negative shift count, an overflowing `<<`, a negative operand of `/` or `%`, a zero divisor, a Bignum.
- The width is the backend's (int_bits, from int_max), not 63: the interpreter's int is 63 bits, and a 32-bit target's is 32.
- int_ops_plain, which says no Integer operator is redefined, now asks after these seven too. A program's `Integer#>>` is still called.
- vm_call answers a send of one of them on two Integers with int_fast_op, before making a scope.

After: `p >> 3; p & 7` is 3.77 G on the evaluator and 3.93 G on the VM.

## The benchmarks (instructions, VM over evaluator)

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| so_nsieve_bits | 1.29x (714 G / 555 G) | 1.09x (559 G / 511 G) |
| so_spectralnorm | 1.13x | 1.06x |
| so_exception | 1.10x | 1.07x |
| so_nsieve | evaluator over 120 s | 0.91x |

Still over 1.05x: app_mandelbrot 1.16x, so_lists 1.14x, so_matrix 1.09x, so_nsieve_bits 1.09x, so_object 1.08x, app_raise 1.07x, so_exception 1.07x, so_spectralnorm 1.06x.

What is left of so_nsieve_bits's gap:
- **An operator send that is not two Integers.** `s == 1` costs the VM 12.2k instructions and the evaluator 6.6k: vm_scoped's region and the EBin made in it. vm_scoped copies the answer out of its region because the scope's arena is compacted when it goes back to the pool (an ASAN-found use after free). A lighter path has to keep that, so it is its own round.
- **`String#[]`.** About 19k instructions a call on both executions: the general cost of a builtin call.

Corpus 809 checks the seven operators over Integers and Bignums of both signs, shift counts past the width, and redefined operators. It gives ruby's output on main's build and on this one: it guards the fast path, it does not show a fix.
