# `==` / `!=` of a builtin value on the VM, answered without a scope when nothing is in the way

The VM's last gap on so_nsieve_bits, and the cost every benchmark still slower on the VM shares: an operator send that is not two Integers. In instructions a call, the VM paid 5.7k more than the evaluator for `s == 1` and 3.3k more for `a == b`. That was:
- vm_scoped's scope from the pool, its region and its try;
- vm_call_in binding the operands;
- the parser's EBin made in the scope.

**Two tries that did not help (both dropped):**
- Sending the operator like any other call (emethod_args) cost more: `s == 1` went from 6.33 G to 7.81 G for 300k calls. EBin's arm is cheaper than the general dispatch for a builtin receiver.
- Asking gc_churn_bytes one time in 16 saved 6.5% of an evaluator loop that makes a String a statement. On the benchmarks it moved nothing by more than 1%, which is not worth changing what the collector reads.

**The change.** For a builtin receiver, EBin's arm ends in eval_bin_prim, which takes the two values. vm_bin_eq_ok asks what the arm asks before that point. When nothing takes the call, vm_call calls eval_bin_prim itself, under the same bi_reg frame. What takes the call stays EBin's:
- a TracePoint, a refinement, an undefined or aliased operator, a moved object;
- a receiver that is not a String, Symbol, nil, true, false, Array or Hash (a number stays EBin's, for coercion);
- a subclass tag or a singleton class (hng_store);
- an `==` or the operator defined on the class or above it, a prepended module's included;
- an argument that is an object (its #to_str, #to_ary, #to_hash or its own #==), a Complex or a class;
- two Arrays or two Hashes that are not identical and hold an object, which the arm compares by the elements' #==.

Nothing is bound into a scope, so nothing comes back out of one: vm_scoped's copy of its answer out of its region has nothing to guard here.

| 300k calls, instructions | evaluator | VM before | VM after |
|---|---|---|---|
| `s == 1` | 4.55 G | 6.33 G | 4.76 G |
| `[1, 2] == [1, 2]` | 5.87 G | 6.85 G | 4.83 G |
| so_nsieve_bits's block | 13.73 G | 16.57 G | 15.02 G |

On the benchmark, so_nsieve_bits went from 1.09x to 1.02x. The others do not compare builtins much and did not move.

Still over 1.05x: app_mandelbrot 1.16x, so_lists 1.14x, so_matrix 1.09x, so_object 1.08x, so_exception 1.08x, app_raise 1.07x, so_spectralnorm 1.06x. `<<` and `+` of builtins are the same shape as `==` and come next, with the checks the review of #182 named: a frozen receiver, Array#<< answering self.

Corpus 810 has the cases that take the call: an argument with #to_str / #to_ary / its own #==, a Numeric that coerces, a subclass's and a singleton's #==, a refinement, a prepended #==, a redefined Array#==, an undefined Symbol#==. It gives ruby's output on main's build too: it guards the fast path. TracePoint's c_call for `==` / `!=` matches ruby on the VM. On the evaluator it fires two more, as on main.
